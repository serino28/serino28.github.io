Antonio Serino

Ph.D. student in AI and NLP at the University of Milan-Bicocca. My research explores the internal mechanisms of Transformer-based language models through mechanistic interpretability, that is, how they construct representations and make decisions, with a particular focus on the of Large Language Models. My goal is to develop robust evaluation and explanation techniques that ensure AI systems remain aligned with human values, with the aim of creating transparent and safe decision-support systems. What I'm focused on

The recent security incident involving an autonomous AI agent at Hugging Face, the first documented large-scale attack orchestrated by an AI agent, has pushed me to focus even harder on AI safety through mechanistic interpretability. I'm investigating localized geometric representations: how safety-relevant features emerge not only in the residual stream, but also within attention-head subspaces and the MLP, and how they propagate through the network to produce misaligned behavior. I'm also exploring whether the representation of certain emotions relates to model alignment. My guiding principle: the more an internal geometric structure can steer a model's behavior, the more it deserves to be interpreted.

Questions I ask myself

Mechanistic interpretability lets us probe a model's internal components to monitor, control, and interpret the geometric structures that drive its behavior. That raises two questions I keep coming back to. On monitoring and control: can we build "transmission" methods across the different internal sites where a feature is represented, tracking and steering it end to end rather than at a single layer? On interpretation: are there real-world LLM applications, in security or other sensitive domains, that genuinely require decision-making, and could they become the bridge between mechanistic interpretability and classical XAI?

Publications

  • Addressing the Concept Faithfulness Gap through Probe-Guided Sparse Autoencoders
    Lorenzo Malandri, Fabio Mercorio, Antonio Serino
    EMNLP 2026 (Findings), Budapest, Hungary

    Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) often produce features that look interpretable but fail to predict task-relevant concepts like toxicity or sentiment, a mismatch we call the concept faithfulness gap. We introduce ProbeSAE, a probing-guided fine-tuning framework that closes this gap, yielding a more monosemantic sparse decomposition that stays causally effective. Evaluated on two SAE architectures and two domains across six languages, despite training only on English data, ProbeSAE learns language-independent, faithful features and passes SAEBench evaluation, ruling out simple overfitting.

  • Safe-Unsafe Concept Separation Emerges from a Single Direction in Language Models Activation Space ↗
    Andrea Ermellino, Lorenzo Malandri, Fabio Mercorio, Antonio Serino
    EACL 2026, Rabat, Morocco

    We identify the layer where safe/unsafe concepts are maximally separable in pretrained LLMs. A simple linear classifier on internal representations provides robust safeguarding without weight modification, validated across 8 domains, 3 tasks, and 16 non-English languages.

  • SFAL: Semantic-Functional Alignment Scores for Distributional Evaluation of Auto-Interpretability in Sparse Autoencoders ↗
    Fabio Mercorio, Filippo Pallucchini, Daniele Potertì, Antonio Serino, Andrea Seveso
    EMNLP 2025 (Industry Track), Suzhou, China

    We propose SFAL, a novel evaluation framework that measures how well LLM-generated feature explanations align with actual feature behavior. Unlike costly simulation-based methods, SFAL uses embedding-based semantic similarity for fast, scalable assessment of auto-interpretability quality.
