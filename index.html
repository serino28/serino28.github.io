Antonio Serino

Hi! I am Antonio, a PhD student in Data Science and Artificial Intelligence at the 🇮🇹 University of Milano-Bicocca, working on the interpretability and safety of large language models. I am currently a Visiting Researcher at 🇸🇬 Nanyang Technological University. My supervisors are Fabio Mercorio and Lorenzo Malandri in Italy, and Erik Cambria in Singapore.

🔬 My research sits at the intersection of mechanistic interpretability, representation engineering and AI safety. I study how language models encode safety-critical features in latent geometric spaces, how these can be faithfully interpreted, and how that geometry can be used to steer and control the internal mechanisms behind a model's behavior.

🎓 I am an Ermenegildo Zegna Founder's Scholar, and I contribute to high-performance computing research on the Leonardo supercomputer, both as Principal Investigator and as a team member.

Outside of research, I enjoy interdisciplinary work and sugar-free coffee.

Selected publications

Addressing the Concept Faithfulness Gap through Probe-Guided Sparse Autoencoders

Lorenzo Malandri, Fabio Mercorio, Antonio Serino

EMNLP 2026 (Findings), Budapest, Hungary · to appear

Sparse Autoencoders often produce features that look interpretable but fail to predict task-relevant concepts, a mismatch we call the concept faithfulness gap. ProbeSAE closes it with probe-guided fine-tuning, yielding more monosemantic, language-independent features that remain causally effective.

Safe-Unsafe Concept Separation Emerges from a Single Direction in Language Models Activation Space

Andrea Ermellino, Lorenzo Malandri, Fabio Mercorio, Antonio Serino

EACL 2026, Rabat, Morocco

We locate the layer where safe and unsafe concepts are maximally separable and show that a simple linear classifier on internal representations safeguards a model without modifying its weights, across 8 domains, 3 tasks and 16 non-English languages.

Towards the Terminator Economy: Assessing Job Exposure to AI through LLMs

Emilio Colombo, Fabio Mercorio, Mario Mezzanzanica, Antonio Serino

IJCAI 2025, Montreal, Canada

We introduce TEAI, a task-level index that uses LLMs to assess how exposed U.S. occupations are to AI automation, enabling granular analysis of workforce vulnerability to AI displacement.
